# How much autonomy should an agent have? — literal revision

The article explicitly describes autonomy as a configurable dial with an owner and a number next to it. This revision makes that metaphor recognizable: one raised mechanical rotary knob on a compact control base, with an aligned indicator, travel arc, rotating stop arm and two fixed end stops. No numerals, unrelated cube, or floating threshold plane are used.

The base is a complete 160 × 20 × 150 solid. A central shaft is anchored six units into the case and meets the knob's underside at Y=8. The knob is a complete 86-unit-diameter, 38-unit-deep cylinder with closed disks. Its top face is the only highlighted part; the outward +Y normal correctly hides that highlight when viewed from below. The indicator on the top and the rigid stop arm below share the same 45° radial direction.

The arm overlaps the knob by five units, extends to radius 66 and sweeps above the case. Fixed end stops at radius 64 define a 160° permitted travel sector from −35° to 125°. Their heights overlap the arm's height, so they are real mechanical stops rather than decorative dots. The engraved travel arc has the same center and radius as the end-stop positions. The current indicator lies between the stops. Recipe assertions check the shaft/knob joint, axis alignment, radius, thickness, arm overlap, case clearance, stop contact range, top-face normal and travel arc.

Inspected all eight rendered views on the final contact sheet: Dark and Light neutral, oblique, back and underside. The neutral view reads as a rotary control with a visible case, top pointer and limits. Oblique and rear views preserve the assembled knob and base; the underside correctly hides the top highlight and most mechanism behind the base. All geometry above the highlighted top face is limited to its own engraved pointer, so no sibling object can incorrectly show through that fill. No mesh edges or disconnected hardware were visible. All mechanical and concept assertions passed. Root applies the final approved backgrounds, Cream/Paper palette and thinner stroke without changing this world geometry.
