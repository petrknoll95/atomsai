Contact us
We kept getting called in after the pilot failed.
AtomsAI started from a pattern we saw repeatedly inside SaaS Labs. Companies had budget for AI, appetite for it, and a pilot that had already impressed a steering committee. What they didn’t have was anything running. The model wasn’t the blocker. The blocker was the twelve systems between a good output and a finished piece of work — and the fact that nobody had scoped the exception queue, the approval gate, or the runbook.
We came at it from the operator side. The group behind us built JustCall and serves more than 6,000 customers, which means we’ve spent years shipping software into other people’s messy, regulated, half-documented workflows and being on call when it breaks. That’s a different instinct from a research lab’s, and it’s the instinct this work needs.
So we set the company up to do both halves. We consult: we tear the workflow down, baseline it, and tell you which ones are worth automating and in what order. Then we build it — the same senior engineers, in your repository, behind your code review, in your standups. The advice is only worth something once it is running, so we stay until it is. The first thing we produce is a ranked list that sometimes tells you not to buy anything yet. The last thing we produce is a handover, because this is your moat — not ours.

There is engineering depth in four time zones, so an on-call commitment is a rota and not a promise. And there are existing partnerships with most of the systems we’ll need to write to, which removes weeks from an integration timeline.

Four rules we hire and fire on.
Consultants who ship.
We give the advice and then we write the code. The senior engineer who scopes the workflow is the one who merges it. Every engagement ends in a running system, not a recommendation — and no discovery phase bills for six weeks before anything works. If a project needs a slide, it’s because a human needs to be convinced, not because we sell slides.
Integration is the product.
The interesting part is never the model. It’s the write-back, the gate, the exception path and the 2am alert. We take pride in the unglamorous half, because that’s the half that decides whether anything ships.
You own the system.
Code, prompts, evals, infrastructure and runbooks land in your repository under your license as we go. Nothing stops working if you stop paying us.
This is your moat — not ours.
Say the uncomfortable number.
If a workflow won’t pay back, we say so before the contract. If access approvals are the critical path, we put it in writing on day one. If we were wrong, we’ll be the ones who raise it.
