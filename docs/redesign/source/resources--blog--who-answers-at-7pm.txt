Your business closes at six. Your customers don’t stop at six. After hours they write on your website chat, email you, text the number on your van, and call. Each of those lands in a different place, and most of it waits until someone gets in the next morning. By then some of those people have booked with whoever answered first. The fix is one agent that answers every channel with the same memory, books what can be booked, wakes someone up only for what’s urgent, and leaves your team one inbox showing everything that happened overnight.

Nothing in that night is unusual. Every step is someone doing their job with the tools they have. The customer chose the channel, and each channel lives in a different tool.
Why do after-hours messages go unanswered?
1. Every channel lands in its own inbox.
Nobody sees all of it, so nobody can answer all of it, and the morning starts with someone hunting through each place in turn. You notice this when a customer says “I messaged you last night” and nobody can find the message.
2. Customers repeat themselves.
The 6:42pm visitor came back at 11:48pm and asked the same question, because nothing remembered the first conversation. The 7:40pm caller tried again at 6:55am. When your team finally replies, different people answer different channels, sometimes with different answers. You notice this when two of your staff reply to the same customer in two places.
3. The first reply gets the first chance.
Someone who needs help tonight may well ask more than one business. If the AC is out and the homeowner messages three companies, whichever answers first gets the first chance to book. A reply at 8:30am, however friendly, competes with someone who answered at 7:13pm. You notice this when a customer replies to your morning message with “Thanks, already sorted.”

What you control.
Which channels the agent answers, during which hours, and in whose name. What it can book, change or cancel, and what needs a person to approve. What counts as urgent: for ex., a no-heat call in January or a gas smell pages your on-call person straight away with a summary, while a price question waits for morning. Your team can pause the agent for one customer or join any conversation live.
What it never does on its own.
Diagnose, quote a price you haven’t approved, promise something your schedule can’t deliver, or argue with a customer. When someone asks for a person, or when the agent isn’t sure, it hands over with a short summary, what it already tried, and the full transcript, so nobody has to ask the customer to start again.
What we connect during your build.
Your calendar (for ex. Google Calendar or Outlook) or booking system, your CRM (for ex. HubSpot or Salesforce), your website, your texting number, your phone line and your email. If you run JustCall, we connect your JustCall account during the build.

Questions buyers ask
Do we really need to answer on every channel?
You need to answer on the channels your customers already use. One agent can cover web chat, SMS, email and phone with the same answers and the same memory, so adding a channel doesn’t mean adding a person to watch it.
Will customers know they’re talking to an AI?
They should, and we set it up that way. The agent introduces itself as your assistant, and anyone who wants a person can ask for one and gets a clear answer about when they’ll hear back.
What happens when something is urgent at night?
You set the rules. For ex., a no-heat call in winter or a burst pipe can page your on-call person straight away with a summary, while a price question waits for a reply in the morning.
Does it book into our own calendar and systems?
Yes. The agent books into the calendar or booking system you already use, updates your CRM, and logs every conversation, so your team starts the morning from one inbox that shows what happened on every channel.
How long does it take to set up?
Standard agents go live in days. Custom work that connects several of your systems takes 4 to 8 weeks.
