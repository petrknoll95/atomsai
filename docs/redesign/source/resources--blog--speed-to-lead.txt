The expensive part of a lead is the wait after it arrives. A lead comes in by ad form, website form, chat, text or phone, then sits in an inbox, the CRM and someone’s to-do list. Hours later a rep calls, on a channel the lead never chose, and gets voicemail. Meanwhile the lead asked two other companies, and one replied at once. The fix is an agent that replies within a minute on the channel the lead used, asks your qualifying questions, books into the right calendar, and keeps following up across channels until the lead answers.

Why do leads go cold before anyone replies?
1. The lead waits in a queue before anyone talks to it.
A form fill turns into an email, the email turns into a CRM record, the record gets assigned, and only then does someone reach out. Each handover is reasonable on its own. Together they add hours. When Harvard Business Review researchers studied how companies respond to online leads, their conclusion was blunt: “most companies are not responding nearly fast enough” (
Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, HBR, March 2011
). Every hour in that queue is an hour in which a competitor can reply first, and the ad money that bought the lead is already spent. You notice this when your CRM shows a first-contact time hours after the lead arrived.
2. You answer on the wrong channel.
Maria typed on her phone during lunch. Company A called her at 4:55pm, in a meeting. Someone who chose to message may not be able to take a call right then, and a callback that lands in voicemail resets the clock. Each missed callback means another attempt, more rep time, and another chance for the lead to book elsewhere. The reply should come where the lead asked, and move to a call only when they want one. You notice this when reps say leads “never pick up”.
3. Follow-up stops at two.
Company A tried one call and one email, then moved on. Leads go quiet for ordinary reasons: a meeting, a school run, a phone left in the car. Without a follow-up schedule that runs on its own, a lead that went quiet once is a lead that’s gone. You paid for every one of those leads, so a lead dropped after one call is ad spend written off, even when the person still needs the job done. You notice this when your CRM is full of leads marked “contacted, no response”.
There is a fourth, quieter cost. When Company A’s estimator finally called, he didn’t know which ad Maria had clicked or what the form said about the leak. She would have had to explain everything again. A rep who walks in knowing the leak, the photo and the ad starts the visit as the company that listened, which is a better start to any estimate.

What you control.
Which channels the agent answers and when. The questions it asks and what counts as qualified. Who gets which leads. The follow-up schedule: how many touches, on which channels, how far apart. Your reps can pause the agent for one lead or join any conversation live.
What it never does on its own.
Quote a price you haven’t approved, offer a discount, promise a date that isn’t in the calendar, or keep messaging someone who said no. When a lead asks for a person, or a question falls outside its rules, it hands over with a summary, a lead score and the full transcript.
What we connect during your build.
Your ad accounts and lead forms, your website forms and chat, your texting number, your phone line, your CRM (for ex. HubSpot, Salesforce or Zoho) and your calendars (Google Calendar or Outlook). If you run JustCall, we connect your JustCall account during the build.

Questions buyers ask
How fast does the agent reply to a new lead?
Within a minute, on the channel the lead used, day or night. A form fill gets a text or a call, depending on your rules; a text gets a text back; a website chat is answered in the chat.
Will it call people who filled in a form?
Only if your rules say so. You can text first and call only when the lead asks for it. The lead can also tap to switch from chat or text to a call, and the agent carries the same conversation over.
Can it book into the right person’s calendar?
Yes. It routes by territory, round robin or account owner, books into that person’s calendar, and sends them a short brief: what the lead asked, their answers, any photos, and the ad they came from.
What happens when a lead goes quiet?
The agent follows up on the schedule and channels you choose, for ex. a text the next morning, an email two days later and a call on day five, and stops the moment they reply or say no.
